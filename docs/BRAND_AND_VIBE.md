# Brand and vibe

A guide for anyone writing, designing, or promoting the product. The reference is the animated page in `frontend/showcase/`. Open it first.

## Who it's for
College students, usually sophomores and juniors, in **any major**. They are short on time, short on money, and apply to many places in bursts (fall for the next summer, and again in January to March). They live in group chats, Reddit, TikTok, Discord, and campus clubs. They have seen plenty of "AI tools" that overpromise.

## Positioning
**The problem:** applying means typing the same answers into dozens of separate employer forms.
**The promise:** Lanyard fills them in for you, then waits for your OK.
**The difference:** it is not "apply to 500 jobs with one click." You review and approve every application, and it will not lie for you. That is the point, not a limitation.

Lead with the pain: *"Stop retyping your GPA into 40 different forms."*

## Messaging pillars
1. **Time back.** The repeated typing goes away. (Use the page's calculator, and keep its "this is an estimate" note.)
2. **You stay in control.** Every answer is shown to you first. You approve or say no.
3. **Any major.** 29 fields, from nursing to engineering to design to policy.
4. **Honest by design.** Lanyard lists what it will not do: send without approval, make accounts, type passwords, get around CAPTCHAs, invent experience.

## Voice
Plain, specific, a little wry. Student to student, not startup to investor.
- Short sentences. Sentence case. Active verbs.
- Say what a button does: "Get early access," not "Submit."
- Name the real thing: "GPA," "work authorization," "the 40th portal."
- Describe, don't hype.

| Say | Don't say |
|---|---|
| Fills out the application, then waits for your OK | Revolutionary AI-powered auto-apply |
| Every answer comes from your resume and profile | Seamlessly unlocks your career potential |
| Some sites forbid automation, so we skip them | Apply to hundreds of jobs with one click |
| An estimate: about 25 minutes by hand, 4 to review | Save 90% of your time |

## Look and feel
- **Mood:** familiar, warm, inviting. Light by default, with a dark mode the visitor can switch to.
- **Accent:** one soft amber (`#e9b872`), like lamplight, on actions. Midnight blues and warm paper tones for everything else.
- **Color has meaning:** amber is a deadline or something waiting on the student, green is approved or done, ember is repeated work or pain. Muted pastel category colors appear only when a field is hovered or chosen.
- **Type:** Bricolage Grotesque for headlines (friendly, a little odd), Public Sans for body text.
- **The signature object:** a name badge on a lanyard that you can drag, swing, and flip. It is the lightest thing on the dark page.
- **Motion with a reason:** a form that fills itself, forty forms collapsing into one profile, a stamp on approval. Every animation shows how the product works. All motion turns off for people who ask for reduced motion.
- **Page order:** pain, then relief, then the product, then an example, then trust, then price.

## Content ideas for JP to build on (no budget needed)
- **Screen recordings** of the hero form filling itself, 8 to 15 seconds, for TikTok, Reels, and Shorts. Hook: "POV: you're applying to your 40th internship."
- **The pain in numbers:** "I typed my GPA into 38 portals." Only use real counts from real people.
- **Reddit and Discord:** useful posts about application tips, with the product mentioned honestly. Read each community's rules about self-promotion first.
- **Campus:** career-center and club announcements, and student ambassadors who get early access.
- **Timing:** recruiting peaks in August to October and January to March. Plan content around those windows.
- **Early-access list:** the sign-up form is a mock until it is connected to a real service. Ask the owner before connecting one.

## Before launching any claim, run this check
- Is it true of what is built *today*, or only of the plan? If only the plan, say "designed to" or leave it out.
- Is there evidence we can show? If not, don't say it.
- Would it encourage a student to break an employer's or a site's rules? If so, cut it.

## The five-second test
Show the page to five students for five seconds, then ask: *"What does this sell?"* If they can't say "it applies to internships for me," fix the hero before anything else. Also ask what they'd pay, and when in the year they'd want it. Write down exact words, since real quotes are the best copy and are the only testimonials allowed here.
